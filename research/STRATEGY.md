# Camp Lindenmere 2027 enrollment strategy

Prepared 2026-11-05. Built on HANDOFF.md (keyword demand), competitors-2026-11-05.md (24 Poconos camps), and CAC-LTV.md (what a camper is worth). Every dollar figure for cost per camper is an estimate until his own tracking replaces it.

## The thesis

A camper is worth $39,000 to $60,000 in lifetime tuition at a 75 percent return rate, and the first summer alone contributes $3,400 to $9,000. Every channel below costs under $2,500 per enrolled camper. Price per camper is not the constraint. The constraints are, in order: how many families are deciding in a given winter, whether the site and offer convert the ones who arrive, and whether the camp keeps the ones it has. So the strategy is ordered the same way. Fix the offer first, then take the Lohikan window, then run the channels that compound (retention, referral, advisors, content), then buy traffic in the months it exists.

## Pillar 1: the offer, before any traffic

Fourteen of 24 competitors have a dated price break and Lindenmere has none. These five changes cost almost nothing and raise the conversion rate of every channel that follows.

1. **A dated early-enrollment credit.** $300 off for enrollment by December 31, $200 by January 31, then full price. It is a deadline, not a discount: the parent was going to buy, this decides from whom and when. Pine Forest and Westmont do exactly this.
2. **A paid two-night trial credited to tuition.** $250 for a Friday to Sunday in late June or a Rookie overnight, credited in full on enrollment. Pine Forest's $200 Explorer weekend is the model. Day-only Rookie Days stay, but the overnight is what converts the parent of a 6 to 8 year old. CAC is the weekend's food and staff, roughly $150 to $300 a family.
3. **A first-timer promise with a number.** Cayuga and Greeley refund prorated tuition if a first-year camper leaves early. Match it. The camp already calls parents a few days in; say so on the page and name the refund.
4. **A visible referral credit, both sides.** $500 to the referring family and $250 to the new one, per enrolled camper. Word of mouth is the top source in every ACA survey and 95 percent of resident-camp parents say they would recommend. The current Refer a Friend page shows no amount.
5. **Director video calls on a published calendar.** Weequahic and Canadensis publish home-visit schedules. A Calendly-style page of 20-minute slots in NYC, Long Island, Westchester, North Jersey and Philly suburbs, plus video, is the conversion event every ad points to. "Book a call" converts a $10,000 purchase; "Enroll" does not.

Site fixes that go with it: answer the affiliation question (40 searches a month ask if the camp is Jewish; whatever the answer, say it), a plain tuition and cost guide page, remove the 2021 COVID link, and date the News posts.

## Pillar 2: the Lohikan window, November to January

Camp Lohikan ran its last season in 2026 and sold to an Orthodox camp. Its NY and NJ families, several hundred of them, need a co-ed Poconos camp for 2027. Lohikan pointed them to two budget camps. "camp lohikan" still gets 3,600 searches a month. This is the cheapest enrollment in the region this winter and it closes by February.

- **Search:** bid on "camp lohikan", "lohikan camp", "camps like lohikan". The name never appears in the ad. Headline: "Co-ed Poconos camp, ages 6 to 16, 2027 enrolling". Expect a few hundred clicks at a few dollars each.
- **A landing page** "For Lohikan families": same region, co-ed, all-choice program, the early credit, a director call link, and the sibling discount. Nothing negative about Lohikan.
- **Meta:** the same page as a retargeting and prospecting ad to parents 35 to 55 in Lohikan's feeder zip codes with the sleepaway camp interest. $1,500 over eight weeks.
- **Direct:** a respectful post from Lindenmere's director in the Lohikan parent and alumni Facebook groups, and a call to Lohikan's director offering to be an option the camp names. Camp advisors who placed Lohikan families are re-placing them now; make sure they have Lindenmere's 2027 dates.
- **Estimated CAC:** $100 to $400 per enrolled camper. Target: 15 to 30 campers.

## Pillar 3: the channels that compound

### Retention

Moving the return rate from 75 to 85 percent adds two summers per camper and is worth more than any acquisition channel. Re-enrollment for 2028 opens on closing day 2027 with a priority rate that expires September 30. Mid-summer parent calls, a camper video on the last day, and a bunk-group re-enrollment incentive ("your whole bunk returns, everyone gets $200") are standard at the premium camps.

### Referral and siblings

With the credit visible, run it as a campaign, not a page: an email to all current families in November and again in January, a referral card in every camper's trunk mail, and a "bring a bunkmate" push for first-year families, who refer most. One in four families adding a sibling raises lifetime value 25 percent; the $200 sibling discount should be on the first screen of the dates page.

### Camp advisors and directories

Tips on Trips and Camps, The Camp Experts, Camp Connection, Student Camp and Trip Advisors, Everything Summer. Free to parents, paid by the camp only on enrollment, a percentage of tuition that none of them publish. Ask the rate, sign with all of them, and send each a dates and rates sheet, the video, and the early credit. This is the only channel with a guaranteed cost per camper and the one Lohikan's families are using right now. Also: ACA Find a Camp, KidsCamps ($99 to $899 a year), MySummerCamps. Cheap, and parents use them to shortlist.

### Camp fairs, January and February

NYC, Long Island, Westchester, and North Jersey fairs are listed in the competitor brief. Competitors attend. Bring the director, the video, a sign-up for a call, and a fair-only credit.

### Day-camp feeder partnerships

Kids age out of NJ, Long Island and Westchester day camps at 10 to 12, and most day camps have no overnight program. Offer six to ten day camps a referral fee per enrolled camper, a Lindenmere night at their family events, and a "graduate to sleepaway" page. Youth sports clubs in the same towns are the same play for the Sports Academy. Nobody in the Poconos is doing this visibly.

### Content and SEO

Each page targets searches with no advertisers and ranks within months. In priority order:

1. The cost guide: "What sleepaway camp costs in 2027", real tuition, deposits, payment plan, sibling discount, what is included.
2. "Is sleepaway camp tax deductible" (320 searches a month, no advertisers). Honest answer, then the payment plan.
3. Age pages: sleepaway camp for 6, 7, 8 year olds, each answering "is my child ready".
4. Eight activity pages: basketball, soccer, volleyball, lacrosse, tennis, horseback, theater, trapeze.
5. "Three weeks or four: which session fits a first-time camper".
6. The packing list page already ranks top ten for twenty queries. Add a 2027 dates call to action and an email capture for the PDF.
7. One dated post a month. Six competitors posted this fall; Lindenmere's News page has none.

### Email and CRM

Every inquiry, tour, Rookie Days registrant and trial family goes into one list with a source tag. A five-email sequence over three weeks: the video, a camper story, the cost guide, the first-timer promise, the deadline. Abandoned CampInTouch applications get a call within 48 hours. The alumni newsletter gets a "your kids are camp age now" issue every January.

## Pillar 4: paid traffic, in the months it exists

Search demand in the four states is 6,000 to 8,000 qualified searches a month off-season and double that January through March. Meta is where camp parents actually are. YouTube is cheap and nobody in the category buys it.

### Google search

The seven-campaign structure in HANDOFF.md: brand always on at a $2 cap; Poconos and PA, home states, near me, activities, cost and sessions, teens and co-ed, all presence-targeted, Display off, partners off, the movie negatives, a 14-day kill rule. Light in November and December, full budget January through March, brand plus activities only April through August. Estimated CAC $450 to $1,350.

### Meta (Facebook and Instagram)

The primary paid channel. Audiences: parents 35 to 55 in the NYC metro, Long Island, Westchester, North and Central Jersey, Fairfield County and the Philly Main Line, with the summer camp and sleepaway camp interests; a lookalike built from the current family list uploaded from CampInTouch; retargeting of every site visitor and video viewer. Creative: the 360 tour, a 30-second "your child chooses their whole day" cut, a camper story, the first-timer promise, and the deadline. Objective: lead form for "book a director call" or "get 2027 dates and rates", never "enroll". Education benchmarks run $26 a lead; at one enrollment per 20 to 25 leads that is $520 to $650 per camper. Run prospecting January through March and retargeting all year.

### YouTube

In-stream ads, geo-targeted to the same areas, placed on sleepaway camp tour videos and on the competitor camps' own channels, which allow it. Cost per view is cents. It is the cheapest way to put the camp video in front of a parent who is researching a rival. $1,000 a month January through March.

### Organic social and short video

Instagram is 6,736 followers, 11th of 24. Only two Poconos camps are on TikTok. The pipeline from the Lowball project applies directly: one storyline at a time, hook scored before filming, real footage, posted through Blotato to Instagram, TikTok and YouTube Shorts, views logged at 24 hours. Summer is the capture season; the winter feed runs on that library. Counselor-made content outperforms camp-made content in this category.

## Budget

Camps typically spend 3 percent of revenue on marketing; the recommendation for overnight camps under 200 campers is 6 to 10 percent. Until his revenue figure is known, the split below is by share. Advisor fees and referral credits are not in it because they are paid only on enrollment.

| Share | Channel |
|---|---|
| 30% | Meta prospecting and retargeting |
| 20% | Google search |
| 15% | Video and content production (summer capture, activity pages, cost guide) |
| 10% | Camp fairs, directories, day-camp partnerships |
| 5% | YouTube |
| 5% | Email and CRM tools |
| 15% | Reserve for whatever is working in February |

## Measurement and kill rules

- One sheet, one row per inquiry, with source, date, tour or call booked, enrolled, session, tuition. If nobody tracked 2026, this is the first job.
- GA4 events for call booked, Rookie Days registered, trial booked, application started. Import only one conversion per lead into Google Ads and Meta; the thank-you page double-counting that bit the photography account is the thing to avoid.
- Monthly: cost per call booked and cost per enrolled camper by channel. Targets: under $150 per call booked, under $1,500 per enrolled camper on paid channels, under $500 on referral and trials.
- Kill rules: a search keyword with click-through under half its ad group median after 14 days is paused; any paid channel over $2,500 per enrolled camper after 60 days of the peak window is cut and the reserve moves to what is under $1,000.
- The one number that matters most is the return rate. Report it every September.

## The first thirty days

1. Get the seven numbers from the camp (return rate, occupancy, revenue per camper, variable cost, 2026 sources, referral credit, starting age).
2. Approve the early credit, the trial weekend, the first-timer promise, and the referral amounts.
3. Build the Lohikan page, the brand campaign, and the Lohikan search and Meta ads.
4. Call the five advisor services and get listed.
5. Publish the cost guide and the tax page. Date the News page.
6. Email current families the referral offer.
7. Set up the inquiry sheet and the GA4 events, then validate the keyword shortlist in Keyword Planner.
